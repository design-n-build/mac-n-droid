# Mac n Droid: video series story bible

A fresh start. Nothing from `video/launch` is reused: not the story, the cast, the look or the rebuilt HTML UI.

**One idea runs through every video:** *Your phone stops pulling you away.*
People with an Android phone and a Mac live with two worlds that don't talk. Every buzz pulls them out of what
they're doing. Mac n Droid brings the phone to the Mac, so they stay in the flow. It's free, with no account and no cloud.

**Who it's for:** Mac users who carry an Android phone. They're tired of emailing files to themselves, picking up the
phone for a code, and paying for apps that do half of this.

**Tone:** calm and confident with a little wit. It's premium but never shouty. Think of an Apple product film's
restraint with a dry, human voice. The motion graphics are big; the UI never bounces or does anything the real app doesn't do.

## 2. The world (shared by every video)

- **Character:** we never see a face. "You" is a pair of hands, a desk and a voice. The VO speaks to the viewer in
  second person, so anyone can be the person in the film.
- **The desk:** a light oak desk by a window. MacBook Air (midnight) open, Pixel phone face-up beside it, a coffee
  mug, a notebook. The light moves through the day across the series: morning, midday, golden hour, night.
- **Devices on screen:** "MacBook Air" and "Pixel 8". The phone's battery reads 82% everywhere.
- **Invented, harmless demo data** (no real people, brands or numbers):
  - Contacts: **Priya** (friend), **Sam** (teammate), **Mum**.
  - Messages: Priya *"Dinner at 8? 🍝"* → reply *"Sounds good, see you there!"*
  - Bank code: **Northwind Bank**, *"482913 is your sign-in code. Never share it with anyone."*
  - Place: **Osteria Lucca, 42 Elm Street**. File: **dinner-menu.jpg**, **Q4-deck.pdf**, **holiday-photos/** (folder).
  - Song: **"Golden Hour Drive" by Lumen Field** (invented).
  - Never show WhatsApp, Spotify, Gmail or other real brands. Use the invented ones or neutral system apps.
- **Two colours, one line:** like the website, everything is black, grey and white. **Green = Android,
  blue = Mac.** Whenever something crosses between the devices, a **dotted line runs from green to blue** (the site's
  motif). That line is the series' signature motion. Every short has at least one crossing.

## 4. Shorts (9:16): the formula

Every short follows the same five beats so the series feels like one family:

1. **Hook (0–1.5 s):** a relatable pain shown, not told, with one bold caption that is a question or a complaint.
2. **Flip (1.5–3 s):** the green→blue line appears and the problem moves to the Mac.
3. **Show (3 s to the end minus 4 s):** the real feature, in the real UI, with one punch-in on the control that matters.
4. **Payoff:** one line of VO that lands the benefit.
5. **End card (3–4 s):** "Mac n Droid · Free for Mac + Android · link in bio", with the same sting every time.

Layout for 9:16: the phone sits on top, the Mac notch or window is cropped and enlarged at the bottom, and the dotted
line runs vertically between them. Keep captions inside the middle 60% (clear of the platform UI at top and bottom).

### S1: "The notch picks up" (25 s)
- **Hook:** the phone buzzing across a desk in a top-down shot. Caption: *"Not now, phone."*
- **Show:** the notification drops from the notch. Then **Reply** typed in the notch, then **Mark as read**, and the
  phone's notification disappears too (show the phone clearing). Last, an incoming call as a notch drop with **Answer / Decline**.
- **VO:** "Your phone's notifications drop right out of your Mac's notch. Reply, mark as read, even decline a call. Dismiss
  it here and it's gone from your phone too."
- **Sounds:** mnd_tap, keyboard, swipe-clear.

### S2: "Codes in one click" (15 s, loop)
- **Hook:** a sign-in page with an empty code field and a blinking cursor. Caption: *"Where's my phone…"*
- **Show:** Northwind Bank drops in → **Copy 482913** → the digits fly into the boxes → a green check. Then show the
  setting that copies codes automatically.
- **VO:** "Sign-in codes from your phone, copied in one click. Or automatically."
- Loop: the end frame is the empty code field again.

### S3: "Copy here. Paste there." (20 s)
- **Hook:** a split screen of the Mac and the phone. Caption: *"Emailing yourself a link? In 2026?"*
- **Show:** the address copied on the Mac shows up in the Android clipboard. A photo copied on the phone is pasted into a Mac
  note. Then the 20-item history fans out on both screens at once.
- **VO:** "Copy text or images on one, paste on the other. Your last twenty copies wait on both. Passwords from your
  password manager never leave the phone."

### S4: "Just drop it" (25 s)
- **Hook:** a USB cable shoved into frame, then flicked away by a cursor. Caption: *"No cables."*
- **Show:** Mac → phone, drag a folder onto the notch (the drop target lights up in the Mac's accent). Phone → Mac, use the **Send files to Mac** Quick
  Settings tile and the files land in Downloads. A Finder **Share → Send to Phone** cameo.
- **VO:** "Drop files or whole folders on the notch, and they're on your phone. Going the other way? One tap. They land in Downloads."

### S5: "Screenshot → paste" (12 s, loop)
- No hook text: a phone screenshot flash, then ⌘V on the Mac, then the image appears. Caption: *"That's it. That's the video."*
- **VO:** "Screenshot on your phone. Paste on your Mac."

### S6: "Don't leave without it" (25 s): story short, most shareable
- **Hook:** a first-person POV of leaving home with keys and laptop bag. Caption: *"Got everything?"*
- **Show:** café, MacBook opens, the real alert **"Did you leave Pixel 8 behind?"** appears. Cut to the phone alone on the kitchen table.
- **VO:** "If your Mac joins another network and your phone's not with you, Mac n Droid lets you know." *(It's based on
  Wi-Fi, not distance. Don't imply GPS or tracking.)*
- **Sound:** mnd_left_behind, played clean.

### S7: "Where's my phone?" (15 s, loop)
- **Hook:** sofa cushions with a faint muffled buzz. Caption: *"It's on silent. Of course."*
- **Show:** the menu bar popover → **Ring** → the cushions bounce to a loud ring (silent mode is shown as on) → **Stop**.
- **VO:** "Ring your phone from your Mac. Full volume, even on silent."

### S8: "Your phone, in a window" (25 s)
- **Hook:** someone reaching for a phone app that only exists on the phone. Caption: *"That app's only on your phone?"*
- **Show:** the Mirror Screen window opens; the cursor scrolls and taps inside the phone; typing with the Mac keyboard.
- **VO:** "Mirror your phone in a Mac window, and control it with your mouse and keyboard."
- Small on-screen note: *"Control needs the optional accessibility setting."* Optional webcam tag: *"Phone as webcam
  over USB, Android 14+."*

### S9: "Texts on your Mac" (20 s)
- **Hook:** a typing marathon with thumbs on a tiny keyboard. Caption: *"Typing essays with your thumbs?"*
- **Show:** the Messages view on the Mac: the thread with Priya, a reply typed fast on a real keyboard, a new conversation from contact search.
- **VO:** "Read and reply to your texts from your Mac. With a real keyboard."

### S10: "Music from either side" (20 s)
- **Hook:** a song playing on the Mac while the hands are on the couch with the phone. Caption: *"Skip. From here."*
- **Show:** the phone's media controls skip the Mac track. The notch live bar with album art and equalizer. The browser extension
  popup controlling a video in the browser.
- **VO:** "Control your Mac's music from your phone and your phone's music from your Mac. Even in the browser."

### S11: "Nobody in the middle" (25 s)
- **Hook:** a cloud icon with an eye, then crossed out. Caption: *"Where does your data go?"*
- **Show:** a top-down "house" diagram with the Wi-Fi dome; the dotted line goes phone → Mac with a lock on it. Real QR
  pairing screens. Labels: *Local Wi-Fi only · QR pairing · AES-256-GCM end-to-end · No account · Open source (GPL-3.0)*.
- **VO:** "Mac n Droid never touches a server. Your phone and Mac pair with a QR code and talk only over your own
  Wi-Fi, encrypted end to end. No account. Free and open source."

### T1: "Set up in two minutes" (YouTube tutorial)
A screen-recording tutorial with a calm voice and chapters: Download → Mac **Open Anyway** → Android install (+ Play
Protect note) → **Pair a Phone** QR → **Finish setup** permissions (+ "Allow restricted settings") → battery
"Unrestricted" on Samsung/Xiaomi → browser extension. Honesty builds trust, so show the scary dialogs and explain them.

## 5. "Real UI, not a mock-up": how to capture it

**Rule: every pixel of app UI comes from the real apps' code.** HyperFrames handles the camera, devices, light,
type, lines and captions. It never redraws app screens. Three ways to get the UI, in order of preference:

### A. Live recordings of the real apps (for anything that moves)
The most realistic option, because the real animations, springs and the notch's own motion are recorded as they are.

**Mac**
- Use a separate, clean macOS user account: default wallpaper or a plain one, no desktop icons, Dock auto-hide,
  Do Not Disturb on (so real notifications can't sneak in), and **Settings → Appearance accent** chosen once for the series.
- Drive the real Mac app with demo data. `tools/FakePhone.java --full` already sends notifications, clipboard and a file
  to the running app. **Extend it with a `--demo <scene>` mode** that sends exactly the demo data above (Priya,
  Northwind 482913, dinner-menu.jpg, the song), so each take is repeatable.
- Record with QuickTime / `screencapture -v` or OBS at **native Retina resolution, 60 fps**, so 2× punch-ins stay sharp.
- The menu bar clock shows the real time. Record near the time the story needs, or crop the menu bar.

**Android** (a spare phone or a clean profile, so no real contacts or messages appear)
- Clean status bar with Android's built-in **System UI demo mode**:
  ```bash
  adb shell settings put global sysui_demo_allowed 1
  adb shell am broadcast -a com.android.systemui.demo -e command enter
  adb shell am broadcast -a com.android.systemui.demo -e command clock -e hhmm 1842
  adb shell am broadcast -a com.android.systemui.demo -e command battery -e level 82 -e plugged false
  adb shell am broadcast -a com.android.systemui.demo -e command network -e wifi show -e level 4
  adb shell am broadcast -a com.android.systemui.demo -e command notifications -e visible false
  ```
- Record with `scrcpy --record=take.mp4 --max-fps=60 --video-bit-rate=24M --no-audio` (or `adb screenrecord`).
- Set the wallpaper or theme so Material You gives the colour you choose for the series (monochrome to match the website is recommended).

### B. Pixel-perfect stills from the code (for exact states and close-ups)
- **Android:** `StoreShots.kt` renders the real Compose screens through Robolectric/Roborazzi. Add a `VideoShots`
  test with the series' demo data, rendered at **3× density**. Because it steps `mainClock` by hand (as the "waiting
  for approval" shot already does), it can also **export real animation frame sequences** (PNG per frame).
- **Mac:** `--store-shots` (`mac/Sources/MacNDroid/Debug/StoreShots.swift`) renders the real SwiftUI views offscreen.
  Add a video mode that renders the notch drop, NotchDrop target, menu bar popover, Messages, Clipboard and the
  left-behind alert at **@2x or @3x**, with transparent backgrounds so they composite cleanly.

### C. Never: HTML rebuilds of app screens
The only things drawn in HTML are the cursor, the green→blue line, titles, captions, diagrams and the end card.

### Hardware and "ultra realistic" finish
- **Device frames:** Apple's official product bezels (Apple Design Resources) for the MacBook, and Google's official
  Pixel frames for the phone. Check each licence allows use in app marketing before publishing.
- **Real-world plates:** shoot the desk, hands, bag, café and sofa yourself on a phone in 4K (free, and more convincing than
  anything generated). Put the captured UI onto the screens in HyperFrames (corner-pin / 3D transform) with matching
  blur, light and reflections.
- **Finish recipe for every screen:** a slight screen glare sweep on camera moves, a soft contact shadow under the devices,
  shallow depth of field on the device not in focus, motion blur on fast moves, and 2–3% film grain on live-action only (never on UI).
- **Masters:** 3840×2160 for YouTube; 1080×1920 for Shorts/Reels; 1080×1350 (4:5) for the Instagram feed. Recompose each
  layout instead of cropping it.

## 6. Sound, music, voice and subtitles

- **App sounds:** use the app's own sounds in `mac/Resources/Sounds/` (mnd_tap, mnd_sent, mnd_received,
  mnd_left_behind). They're original, so they're safe. **Don't use Apple's or Google's system sounds.**
- **SFX:** foley-style buzzes on wood, keyboard, trackpad clicks, whooshes on line crossings and soft risers into
  reveals. Use free libraries (Pixabay, Freesound CC0, or `/media-use`).
- **Music:** one licensed track family for the whole series (same artist or pack), so the shorts feel related. Check that the
  licence covers YouTube **and** Instagram. Duck the music −10 dB under the VO.
- **Voice:** one narrator for every video: warm, mid-pace (~150 wpm) and conversational. Record it yourself or generate it, but
  keep the same voice across the series. Final loudness: −14 LUFS for YouTube and Instagram.
- **Subtitles:**
  - Shorts: burned in, word-by-word highlight (white words, the active word green or blue for the device being talked about),
    max 2 lines, inside the safe zone.
  - YouTube hero and tutorial: clean burned-in subtitles plus an uploaded `.srt` (good for search and accessibility).
  - Captions match the VO exactly. Titles on screen are separate and shorter.

## 7. Claims: say only what's true

- ✅ Free, no account, no cloud, local Wi-Fi only, QR pairing, AES-256-GCM end to end, open source (GPL-3.0).
- ✅ Mac (macOS 14+) and Android 10+. Windows and Linux are **beta**; say so whenever they appear.
- ⚠️ Left-behind alerts are **Wi-Fi based**, not distance or location based.
- ⚠️ Mirroring *control* and automatic phone clipboard need the optional accessibility setting. Webcam is over USB and needs Android 14+.
- ⚠️ Google Play: say **"Launching soon on Google Play"** (or "Download for Android" for the APK). Never say it isn't live
  or isn't available yet.
- ❌ Don't say "notarized", "works with iPhone", "no permissions needed", or anything about battery life you haven't measured.
- ❌ No Apple or Google logos as graphics, no real third-party app branding, no real phone numbers or people.

## 8. How to hand this to Claude Code (Fable 5.2)

Build in this order, one approval step at a time:
1. **Capture kit first:** add the `FakePhone --demo` scenes, the Android `VideoShots` test and the Mac video-shots mode.
   Approve the real UI captures before any motion work.
2. **Hero film storyboard:** stills for each beat in the section 3 table. Approve them.
3. **Hero film build** in a new HyperFrames project at `video/series/hero/`, then the VO, captions and mix.
4. **Shorts template:** one shared 9:16 component (hook caption, the line, the end card), then S1 to S11 as episodes.
5. **T1 tutorial** last. It's mostly a straight screen recording with chapters.

> Read `video/series/STORY.md`. Start with section 5: build the capture kit so every app screen in the series comes
> from the real Mac and Android code with the demo data in section 2. Show me the captures before animating anything.

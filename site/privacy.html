Mac n Droid

Privacy Policy

Effective 10 November 2026 · Applies to the Mac n Droid Android app, Mac app and browser extension.

The short version

  • We collect nothing. Mac n Droid has no servers, no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no crash reporting.
  • Your phone and your Mac talk directly to each other over your own Wi-Fi, encrypted with a key only those two devices know.
  • Your messages, notifications, files and clipboard stay on your own devices. We never see them, store them or sell them.
  • The browser extension talks only to Mac n Droid on the same Mac. It doesn't track what you browse.
  • Mac n Droid is free and open source, so anyone can check that all of this is true.

Who we are

Mac n Droid is a free, open-source project that connects an Android phone to a Mac. "We" in this policy means the developer of Mac n Droid. The source code is published under the GNU General Public License v3.0.

What we collect

Nothing. We don't run any servers, so there is nowhere for your information to be sent to us. Mac n Droid doesn't use accounts or sign-ins, and doesn't include advertising, analytics, tracking or crash-reporting code. We don't sell, rent, share or trade any information about you, because we never have it.

How Mac n Droid works

When you pair your phone with your Mac by scanning a QR code, the two devices create a shared secret key. From then on they find each other on your local network and connect directly. Everything they send is encrypted with AES-256-GCM using that key, so other devices on the network can't read it. Nothing goes through the internet or through us.

Mac permissions

  • Local Network, to find and connect to your phone.
  • Notifications, to show your phone's notifications.
  • Automation (asked the first time you use it), to read the address of the page open in your browser when you send it to your phone, and to play, pause and skip Music or Spotify from your phone.

Browser extension

The Mac n Droid browser extension lets you control music and videos playing in your browser from your phone, and open pages and links on your phone. To do that it:

  • Reads the title, artist and artwork of media playing in a tab, and whether it's playing, so they appear in your phone's media controls.
  • Reads the address of the current page or a link, only when you click the toolbar button or choose Open on Your Phone from the right-click menu.
  • Sends this only to the Mac n Droid app on the same computer (127.0.0.1), which passes it to your phone over your encrypted connection.

It doesn't read your browsing history, the contents of the pages you visit, passwords, forms or cookies, and it never sends anything to us or to anyone else. It needs access to all websites only so it can find music and videos playing on any site you use.

The few times Mac n Droid uses the internet

Mac n Droid itself doesn't need the internet. A few things do connect to other services, and none of them include your personal information:

  • Mac app updates. The Mac app checks GitHub for new versions. Like any website, GitHub sees your Mac's IP address. You can turn automatic checking off in Settings → General.
  • Album artwork. To show artwork, the Mac app may download the cover of the song playing in Spotify from Spotify, and the browser extension may download the artwork of a playing video from the website that's already showing it.
  • QR code scanning on Android. Scanning the pairing code uses Google's code scanner, part of Google Play services. It runs on your phone and Google doesn't receive the image or the code, but Google collects basic diagnostic information about it (such as device model, Android version and error codes) to keep it working. See Google's ML Kit data disclosure.
  • App stores. If you download Mac n Droid from Google Play, the Chrome Web Store, Microsoft Edge Add-ons or Firefox Add-ons, those stores handle the download under their own privacy policies.

Security

Pairing needs physical access to both devices, to scan the QR code. After that, every connection is encrypted and authenticated with the key created during pairing, and a device that hasn't been paired can't connect. The browser extension's link is open only to the same computer, and only to browser extensions, not to websites.

Your choices

  • Turn off any feature, or deny any permission, at any time. Everything else keeps working.
  • Clear your clipboard history on either device.
  • Unpair your phone to stop all syncing and delete the pairing key.
  • Uninstall the app or extension to delete everything it kept on that device.

Since we don't hold any of your information, there's nothing for us to access, correct, export or delete on our side. Everything is on your own devices and under your control.

Children

Mac n Droid isn't directed at children under 13, and it doesn't knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy
